Migraine occurs due to excessive constriction and relaxation of arteries in the brain beyond normal levels. This triggers severe headaches, often accompanied by nausea and vomiting. In certain cases, patients may see flickering lights as an "aura" before the headache begins.

## Identifying Your Triggers

- Sleep disruption (too much or too little sleep)
- Skipping meals or fasting
- Dehydration
- Hormonal changes in women
- Stress and anxiety
- Certain foods and drinks (alcohol, caffeine, processed foods)
- Sensory overload (bright lights, loud sounds, strong smells)

## When to See a Specialist

Consult a neurologist if you experience migraines more than **4 days per month**, if headaches are becoming more frequent or severe, or if over-the-counter medications are no longer effective.
