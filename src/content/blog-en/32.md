Many people believe migraine headaches stem solely from brain abnormalities. However, external factors such as muscle tension, shoulder stiffness, and office syndrome can also trigger migraines.

## Office Syndrome vs. Migraine Headaches

**Office Syndrome headaches** occur when neck and shoulder muscles tighten, restricting blood flow to the head — producing throbbing pain that may affect one or both sides.

**Migraine headaches** involve one-sided pain from temporary chemical imbalances in the brain, causing blood vessels to constrict and dilate excessively, producing throbbing sensations with nausea and light sensitivity.

## Common Migraine Triggers

1. Bright light, flickering lights, or intense sunlight
2. Excessive heat or humidity
3. Office syndrome — prolonged computer use causing neck muscle tension
4. Specific foods (cheese, alcohol, MSG, caffeine)
5. Stress, insufficient rest, and sleep deprivation
6. Estrogen fluctuations — rapid drops trigger "menstrual migraines"
7. Postpartum period — hormone shifts increase migraine frequency

## Treating Migraines with Shockwave

Shockwave therapy stimulates neck and shoulder muscle relaxation effectively. For optimal results, patients should identify headache triggers and **combine physical therapy with medical treatment**.
