Following a cerebrovascular event, some stroke patients frequently experience pain complications. These may arise from abnormal posture, brain injury, reduced muscle and body flexibility, lack of movement, or medication side effects.

## Spasticity and Contracture Pain

Neurological damage from stroke can cause loss of muscle control, leading to muscle stiffness, joint contractures, and spasticity. Untreated prolonged spasticity may result in shortened muscles, muscle weakness, and permanent joint contractures.

## Shoulder Pain and Frozen Shoulder

Shoulder pain commonly affects the weaker side post-stroke. Weakened shoulder muscles subject the joint to gravitational forces, damaging joint tissue and tendons — resulting in shoulder inflammation and frozen shoulder.

## Central Nervous System Pain

Stroke damages specific brain areas, causing abnormal pain perception on the affected side — manifesting as burning, throbbing, sharp, stabbing, or numb sensations. Treatment combines medication with electrical therapy.

## Limb Swelling Pain

Prolonged immobility impairs circulation, causing fluid and blood retention in extremities, leading to swelling and pain. Elevation and physical therapy programs help.
