**Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis)** is a disease causing chronic pain from inflammation of the shoulder joint capsule, making the shoulder stiff and preventing efficient movement. The exact cause remains unclear, though it typically follows shoulder injuries or trauma.

## Who Is at Higher Risk?

Those with **diabetes, autoimmune diseases, rheumatoid arthritis**, and connective tissue disorders have significantly higher rates. Women aged 40–60 are also more commonly affected.

## The Four Stages

- **Painful Stage:** Primarily shoulder pain, possibly with slight stiffness — within the first three months
- **Freezing Stage:** Increased stiffness with persistent pain when moving in restricted directions
- **Frozen Stage:** Stiffness pronounced; substantially impacting dressing, reaching behind the back, or raising the arm
- **Thawing Stage:** Pain decreases and movement gradually improves

## Treatment Options

Anti-inflammatory medications, surgery, or physical therapy — with **physical therapy increasingly preferred** for avoiding surgical intervention and using therapeutic equipment and exercises to restore range of motion.
