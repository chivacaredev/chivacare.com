These conditions relate to dysfunction in controlling oral muscles, requiring coordinated function of the brain, nerves, and multiple muscle groups.

## Common Symptoms

- **Stiff tongue with limited movement:** Abnormal muscle tension reduces directional control, affecting speech and swallowing
- **Unclear speech, weak voice:** Imprecise muscle control (Dysarthria) causes unintelligible speech
- **Difficulty swallowing, easy choking:** Uncoordinated muscles may allow food to enter the airway
- **Chewing difficulties:** Reduced coordination between tongue, cheeks, and jaw
- **Uncontrolled drooling:** Reduced automatic swallowing control causes saliva accumulation
- **Fatigue during speech or eating:** Muscles work inefficiently and tire easily

## Potential Causes

- Stroke affecting speech and swallowing control regions
- Parkinson's disease causing slowness and rigidity
- Cerebral palsy delaying oral muscle development in children
- Head or brain injuries
- Muscle weakness disorders

## Why Occupational Therapy?

Occupational therapists specialize in restoring functional abilities for daily living — emphasizing "returning to actual life functioning." They provide comprehensive assessment, individualized programs, oral motor training, safe swallowing techniques, and caregiver guidance.

## When to Seek Expert Evaluation

Seek evaluation immediately if you notice sudden speech changes, coughing after swallowing, frequent choking, unexplained weight loss, or have a history of stroke. Early assessment enables faster recovery and reduces complication risks.
